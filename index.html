What this is

Transmission lines are often taught as a set of rules handed down: here is a reflection coefficient, here is a standing wave ratio, here is a chart covered in circles. Learned that way the formulas are arbitrary and the factors of two feel like something to memorise rather than something to understand.

They are not arbitrary. Each one follows from the one before it, and the chain is short enough to hold in your head end to end.

The through-line

A wire long enough to matter → a distributed circuit model → two travelling waves → reflection at a mismatch → standing waves → impedance that depends on where you stand → a chart that makes that dependence geometric → networks that cancel it.

Each chapter runs on two tracks. The main narrative is written to be read straight through without heavy mathematics. Wherever a step needs vector calculus or a long algebraic grind, that work sits in a collapsible block you can open or skip without losing the thread. Chapters close with a few conceptual self-checks — questions aimed at misunderstandings rather than at missing formulas.

How to use it

Wherever a formula has a parameter, the figure beside it has a control for that parameter. Drag it.

Distances are in wavelengths throughout, so nothing depends on a particular frequency, and lines are lossless unless a chapter says otherwise.

The chapters are in order, but each one names what it assumes at the top, so you can start in the middle and reach back for what is missing.
